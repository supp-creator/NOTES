* Overview

Use a *T-Table* to look up the probability percentage on the outer edges to find the critical T-value inside the chart.

Use this instead of a Z-table when n < 30.

* Degrees of Freedom (/d f/)

\[
d f = n - 1
\]

Where n is the sample size.

* Using a T-table

Choose your tail and alpha level (\alpha).

Alpha (\alpha) is the significance threshold, usually 0.05 (5%) or 0.01 (1%).

One-tailed vs two-tailed tests directional change. If it is greater or if there is any change at all.


After each metric is chosen, look up the value in all their intersections on the table. That is the *critical T-value*.


* Interpretation

Whether the T-value is positive, negative, or zero has the same interpretation as if a Z-value is positive, negative, or zero.

That is above average, below average, or exactly average, respectively.
