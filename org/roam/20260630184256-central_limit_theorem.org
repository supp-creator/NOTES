* Overview

The *Central Limit Theorem* is the foundation of inferential statistics. It states that for a sufficiently large random sample from any population, the distribution of the sample means will form a normal distribution, regardless of the original population's shape.

This happens typically at n \ge30.

* Pillars of the Central Limit Theorem

1. *The shape is normalized*.

2. *The center stays the same*. The average of all the sample means (\mu_{X}) will equal the true underlying population mean (\mu).

3. *The spread shrinks.* The variation among the sample means gets narrower. This spread is called the *Standard Error*.

    \[
    \text{Standard Error (SE) } = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}
    \]


* Standard Error

A measurement of how much your entire sample mean is expected to fluctuate if the study is repeated with different groups.

A smaller SE means the sample is highly representative of the population.
