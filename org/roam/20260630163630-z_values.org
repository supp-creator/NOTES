* Overview

A *Z-value* measures how many standard deviations a specific data point is away from the mean.

The *Z-table* is a reference chart that converts Z-values into a percentage, showing the exact area under the normal curve to find probabilities.

A Z-value standardizes any normal distribution into a *Standard Normal Distribution* where the mean is always 0 and the standard deviation is always 1.


*Positive Z-value* means that the data point is *above* the average.

*Negative Z-value* means the data point is *below* the average.

*Zero Z-value* means the data point is *exactly equal* to the average.


In general:

\[
Z = \frac{\text{Target Value} - \text{Difference of Mean}}{\text{Standard Error}}
\]


** If n = 1:

\[
Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}
\]


** If a single sample group:

\[
Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}}
\]

Use this when you take one random sample of size /n/ and want to find the probability of that sample's average ($\bar{X}$).

** Multiple Sample Groups (Two Independent Samples)

\[
Z = \frac{(\bar{X_{1}}-\bar{X_{2}})-(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})}{\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}}{n_{2}}}}
\]

Use this when you are comparing the averages of two completely separate groups to see the probability of the difference between them ($\bar{X_{1}}-\bar{X_{2}}$).



* Central Limit Theorem

As the sample size grows, its distribution naturally becomes normal regardless of the shape of the underlying population.

When sample size is large (n \ge 30), use the Z-table.

If sample size is small (n < 30) but the population standard deviation (\sigma) is known and that the population is normally distributed, use the Z-table.


* Using a Z-table

First, calculate your Z-score.

Which is X minus the mean and all that is divided by the standard deviation. Just make sure the final Z-Score is up to the hundredths place.

Secondly, split the Z-Score between up to the tenths place and the hundredths place.

The first two digits of the Z-Score is the row value while the last digit is the column number.

** Interpretation

The value you find:

- To find the probability of scoring *less* than your value: keep the table value as-is.
  
- To find the probability of scoring *more* than your value: Subtract the table value from 1: (1 - Z) \cdot 100%
  
- To find the probability *between* two different Z-scores: look up both scores in the table individually and subtract the smaller percentage from the larger percentage.
